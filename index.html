Document This Process ▾

The average person thinks up to 70,000 thoughts a day your mind is constantly processing, reflecting, and moving. Because of this, it can be hard to clearly see how much progress you're actually making.

That's why I highly recommend documenting your experience whether that's in a notebook, here in this tracker, or another format that works for you. Some people write short reflections. Others do video diaries, voice memos, or just talk out loud.

Take note of how you feel before and after your meditation, and how your experience changes from day to day. Over time you may notice subtle shifts feeling calmer, more present, or more aware of your thoughts and reactions.

Tap any day cell above to add your notes, reflections, and observations.

Meditation Posture ▾
  • Seated posture
  • Spine straight
  • Cross legged

Why seated and not lying down? Not only do you most likely not sit in this position for anything else (Pavlovian effect in play here too), but I've been to too many meditation classes where the person next to me is literally falling asleep. For this practice you want to instill mindfulness, presence, and discipline. That quiet is where you access your higher self.

What do I do with my body? ▾

There are all types of discomforts that become louder the quieter you get. Sitting in your body with no external distractions can make you notice things that feel misaligned, tense, maybe even slightly painful.

Remember, meditation is a practice. Being still seems easy but if it's not what you're used to, like anything else it becomes something you simply practice and get better at.

Your foot may fall asleep, you may need to scratch an itch. What matters most here is awareness practicing ignoring your body's every last impulse to move around.

If there is one thing I highly recommend not to negotiate with it's opening your eyes. You will, and that's okay, but with time you learn it's all in your head. Your body is getting used to this.

Something that really helped me fight the urge to move is swaying if I absolutely needed to move I'd sway in circles, move my head around, or shake my head while keeping my eyes closed. It quite literally looks like you're fighting yourself. And honestly, you are and you're winning.

What is Meditation Anyway? ▾

Meditation is the practice of training your attention. Where attention goes, energy grows.

It is one of the oldest human practices on record, found across virtually every culture, spiritual tradition, and philosophy in history. Modern neuroscience has since confirmed what ancient practitioners discovered through direct experience: sitting in deliberate stillness changes the brain, the nervous system, and the quality of your inner life.

This tracker focuses on stillness and mindfulness meditation the foundational practice. If you can learn to be still and present, every other form of meditation becomes available to you.

So if you've ever sat down to meditate and thought "I'm doing this wrong" you weren't. You were just beginning.

Why Practice Stillness? ▾

Because stillness is the foundation everything else is built on.

In stillness, you create the conditions for real self-awareness. You stop reacting and start choosing. You stop being pulled by every thought and start observing them. The nervous system learns to regulate itself. The mind learns it is not its thoughts.

Most people have never actually sat in complete stillness for more than a few minutes without a screen, a podcast, or a task. This practice asks you to do exactly that and what comes up in that space is often the most honest information you have access to.

Create a Mental Anchor ▾

A common misconception is that you have to "clear your mind" think no thoughts, silence everything. But will that actually work?

I personally prefer to create a mental anchor. An anchor is a single point of focus your mind can return to whenever it wanders. It could be your breath, a word, a visualization, a sensation, or a sound.

The key is not to never lose your focus it's to notice when you've lost it and gently come back. That moment of returning? That is the practice. Every time you notice and return, you are strengthening a mental muscle.

FAQs ▾

What time of day is best to meditate?

Morning is often ideal because the mind is quieter before the day begins. However, the best time is simply the time you can stay consistent with.

Is it normal to feel nothing at all?

Yes. Meditation does not always produce dramatic feelings or breakthroughs. Many of the benefits happen gradually and subtly over time.

What if I miss a day?

That's completely okay. Simply continue with the next day. Meditation is about consistency over time, not perfection.

Can I use this beyond 30 days?

Absolutely. This meditation can be used as long as you desire. More meditations are available at thetinycosm.com if you wish to switch things up.
